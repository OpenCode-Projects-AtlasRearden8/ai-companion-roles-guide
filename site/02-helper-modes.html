The Five Helper Modes

Most of what you will ever want from an AI fits into five modes. Think of them as the five stools at the counter. Learn these and you can get by — learn to pick them on purpose and you are off the beginner slope for good.

Each mode below gets: what it is, when to grab it, a sample opening, and the one way people usually misuse it.

1. The Brainstormer

What it is: an idea machine with no investment in your dignity. Its whole job is volume and variety, and it should never judge.

Grab it when: you have a blank page, a naming problem, a "there must be other options I haven't thought of" feeling.

Sample opening:

You are my brainstormer. Rules: give me 12 ideas, numbered, one line each.
Wild ideas welcome. Do not explain any of them. Do not say which are best —
I'll pick.

Common misuse: letting it polish the first idea instead of making more ideas. If you catch yourself saying "make that one better," you have switched to the Editor — go announce it (more on that in rotating roles).

2. The Editor

What it is: eyes only for your words. It reads what you wrote and marks it up — clarity, length, tone — without rewriting your voice into robot-speak.

Grab it when: you have a draft. Any draft: email, essay, dating profile, cover letter, apology text to your landlord.

Sample opening:

You are my editor. Be honest and brief. Here are your marks:
- [FIX] for confusing sentences
- [CUT] for anywhere I repeat myself
- [ASK] for where you'd need more info
Mark up the draft below. Show me at most 10 marks, most important first.
Do NOT rewrite whole paragraphs unless I ask.

Common misuse: asking for "feedback" — a word with no edges. Every kind of feedback says yes to everything. Pick the marks (as above) and you will get marks instead of a lecture.

3. The Planner

What it is: the one that turns a fog of wanting into an ordered list with steps. It thinks in sequences, weeks, and "what has to happen first."

Grab it when: a task feels big, scary, or vague: moving apartments, job hunting, planning a trip, prepping for a certification, organizing any kind of paperwork pile.

Sample opening:

You are my planner. Task: get my motorcycle permit this spring.
Break it into steps, newest-to-now: what to do this week first.
Flag anything that costs money or a deadline. Keep steps under 10.
Ask me nothing yet — make your best assumptions, I'll correct after.

Common misuse: letting it plan your values for you. A planner is great at ordering steps and terrible at knowing your life. State the assumption rule (as above) so it never stalls asking you questions you did not share material for.

4. The Explainer

What it is: the patient, plain-English teacher. It takes any topic and says it the way a friend would at dinner — no jargon, no condescension, no eleven caveats.

Grab it when: you are confused, you read something that went over your head, or you need a concept explained at your level, not an expert's.

Sample opening:

You are my explainer. Explain [topic] in plain English, like I'm smart
but new. Rules: no jargon — if a term is unavoidable, define it the
moment you use it. One example from everyday life. Under 200 words.
Then ask me if you should go one level deeper.

Common misuse: using it for things needing accuracy over clarity — dosing questions, tax law, meds. Clarity is the Explainer's style, not a guarantee of truth. When the stakes rise, the explainer's best move is helping you phrase questions for a professional.

5. Drill Sergeant-Lite

What it is: a routines coach with the volume turned to 3 out of 10. It checks in, keeps score, nags warmly, and — this is the trick — stays kind when you fall off.

Grab it when: you want help keeping a routine: drinking water, stretching on night shifts, practicing a language 10 minutes a day, studying for a big exam in small bites.

Sample opening:

You're my routine coach. I want to stretch for 10 minutes before every
shift. Style: drill sergeant, but volume at 3/10. Each check-in:
1 question, 1 encouragement, 1 next step. If I miss two days,
do NOT guilt-trip me — reset me gently and shrink the goal.
Today is day 1. Check in with me right now.

Common misuse: pulling your real mental health into it. This mode is brilliant for habits and out of its depth for low seasons. Those are for a professional, and a good routine coach role says so — you can even put that in the boundary line.

How the five fit together

Notice the pattern: every mode has a name, a job, and edges. That is the role, job, boundary habit in disguise.
