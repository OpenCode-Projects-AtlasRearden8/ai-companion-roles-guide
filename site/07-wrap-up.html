A Gentle Wrap-Up

That is the manual. Here is the memory-size version, and the two weeks after it.

The whole skill, in five sentences

  1. Name the role ("You are my editor…").
  2. Give one job ("…tighten this paragraph…").
  3. Set one boundary ("…under 80 words, no lecture.").
  4. When the answer drifts — in size, style, or honesty — say so in one line and continue.
  5. When the task changes nature, rotate: "New role. You are now my…"

That's it. Everything else — the five modes, the fifteen boundary recipes, the thirty cards — is scaffolding for those five sentences.

A two-week plan for making it stick

Week 1 — pick two cards. Choose one Creator (Brainstormer is fine) and one Minders card that matches your actual life. Use each three times. Do not evaluate; just notice where it worked.

Week 2 — one rotation. Find a task big enough for two hats (a piece of writing, a plan with a decision in it). Run the rotation sentence once, deliberately, and audit the handoff.

That is the entire training program. It fits in two weeks of a busy life — it fits night-shift schedules, small kids, low-energy weeks. Small is not laziness, it is design; see the Low-Energy Day Coach.

Where you can go next

  • The card deck — keep browsing. It rewards rereading, because different cards click at different life-moments.
  • The print workbooks — open them in a browser, print them, pen them. site/workbooks/ if you are reading the source.
  • Rewrite a card into your own words. The moment a role's opening sounds like something you would say, it stops being a technique and becomes a tool.

The honest limits

  • AI roles are tools for thinking and organizing. They are not clinicians, lawyers, or financial planners, and no card in this deck is a license to skip those humans.
  • Long conversations will drift. Expect it. The saves in this manual exist because drift is normal, not because you used it wrong.
  • Your best prompts will be the ones you wrote yourself. Treat every opening line here as a starting sketch, never a verdict.

Go pick a role. You will be surprised how much better the answers get — and how much quieter the guesswork.
