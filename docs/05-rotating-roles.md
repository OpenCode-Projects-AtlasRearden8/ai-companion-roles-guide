# Rotating Roles Mid-Chat

A great AI conversation is often not one role — it is a *sequence*. Draft with the Brainstormer, shape with the Editor, schedule with the Planner, all in one chat. The trick is making the rotation clean, so the last conversation does not drag the assumptions of the first one behind it.

## When to rotate

These moments usually call for a new hat:

- The **task's nature changed**, not just its content. Ideation → writing → planning. That is three modes.
- You catch yourself **asking a different kind of question.** "Is this sentence good?" to a Brainstormer is a sign you need an Editor.
- The answer **satisfies you but isn't what the role is for.** A Brainstormer's list is done being useful once you have picked one — that is the Editor's starting gun.

## The rotation sentence

One clean sentence does it every time:

```
New role. You are now my [role], with one job: [job]. Forget the last
role's rules except [anything to keep]. Start by [first action].
```

Example, one conversation, three hats:

```
Turn 1:  You are my brainstormer. 15 one-line ideas for a name for my
         reselling side business. No explanations.

Turn 7:  New role. You are now my editor. Here's my one-paragraph shop
         description built on idea #4. Mark [FIX]/[CUT]/[ASK], max 6 marks.

Turn 12: New role. You are now my planner. Turn this launch into 5 steps
         starting this weekend. Flag costs and deadlines.
```

Nothing restarts, nothing gets lost, and each role runs with clean edges.

## The carry-over rule

When you rotate, say what to *keep*:

- **Keep the facts.** Everything established (the topic, your constraints, the decision so far) stays.
- **Keep chosen references carefully.** If the Editor suggested a phrase you liked, carry it forward on purpose.
- **Drop the old job's agenda.** The Brainstormer wants more ideas. Tell the new Planner to not propose ideas: "new role" plus "no new ideas" — the missing boundary everyone forgets.

## The handoff audit

After any rotation, spot-check the first reply with the audit from [when roles break](04-when-roles-break.md):

> Job (what), style (how), size (how much) — still matching?

If the new role is leaking old habits ("the editor is still listing ideas"), that is not a failure — that is the AI hedging around its uncertainty. Re-state the boundary once, concretely.

## A full rotation walk-through

Realistic arc for planning a shift-change swap with your coworker:

```
1. BRAINSTORM:  10 options for trading my next three Fridays.
2. EDIT:        Cut to the 2 workable ones. Point out any that would
                break our unit's coverage rules — flag, don't decide.
3. PLAN:        Step plan for proposing option A to my manager.
4. EXPLAIN:     Plain words for how staffing ratios work, education only.
5. DRILL-3/10:  Sweet, now I have a draft message. Remind me tomorrow
                to send it if I chicken out.
```

Five hats, one thread, zero context reset. (And notice the staffing question stayed *education-only* — that is the boundary habit from [boundaries](03-boundaries.md), doing quiet work.)

## What not to chain together

Some pairs rub:

- **Editor after bottom-fishing.** If you are still raw about a writing piece, corrections sting no matter how they are worded. Cheerleader first, Editor after.
- **Planner while still deciding.** A plan is only as good as the decision under it. Settle *what*, then plan *when*.
- **Drill Sergeant-Lite on a hard day.** Routines coach is for maintenance mode. Hard days need a smaller ask, not a bigger drill.
