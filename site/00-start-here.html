AI Companion Roles Guide

Start Here

If you have ever typed a question into an AI and gotten an answer that was technically correct but totally unhelpful — this manual is for you. No shame in it. It happens because of the most common mistake in beginner AI use:

Asking without giving a role.

You would not ask a stranger at a bus stop the same way you ask your best friend, your editor, or your drill sergeant. You tune your ask to who is listening. AI works the same way. When you say "help me with my essay," it has to guess which helper you need. When you say "You are my editor. Be honest, be brief, mark up my draft below," it stops guessing and starts helping.

What this guide will do for you

By the end of this manual you will be able to:

  1. Pick a helper mode on purpose instead of hoping the AI guesses right.
  2. Set boundaries in your prompt so answers come out the right length, tone, and shape.
  3. Spot the moment a role breaks — when the AI drifts into jargon, over-helps, or quietly changes jobs mid-chat.
  4. Rotate roles in one conversation without starting over.
  5. Grab any of 30 starter roles from the card deck, each with a sample opening line you can paste today.

One promise and one disclaimer

The promise: everything here is in plain English. If a word like "system prompt" shows up, we explain it like a friend would, in the same breath.

The disclaimer: this guide is education only. It is never medical, legal, or financial advice. Some role cards touch on habits, budgeting, studying, and health literacy — those are for learning and organizing, not for self-treatment, self-diagnosis, or anything a licensed professional should handle. When in doubt, the role's job is to help you prepare a better question for the professional, not to answer it.

The 30-second version

If you read only one paragraph today, read this one:

Vague ask in, vague answer out. Before you hit send, name the role ("You are my editor…"), state the job ("…make this shorter without losing the point…"), and set one boundary ("…in fewer than 100 words. No new topics."). That three-part habit — role, job, boundary — fixes about 80% of disappointing AI conversations.
